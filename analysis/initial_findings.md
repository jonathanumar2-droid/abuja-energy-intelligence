# Initial Findings and Descriptive Analysis

## Kaduna–Abuja Corridor Energy Intelligence Project

### 1. Introduction

This document presents preliminary descriptive findings from the initial community renewable energy infrastructure dataset.

The dataset contains 17 community energy system records with information on solar photovoltaic capacity, MPPT capacity, battery storage and inverter capacity.

The objective of this initial analysis is to examine the distribution of available system capacities and identify areas requiring further investigation.

The findings are descriptive and do not represent an independent technical assessment of operational system performance.

## 2. Dataset Overview

The initial dataset contains:

- Number of community records: 17
- Total recorded PV capacity: 814.90 kWp
- Total recorded MPPT capacity: 738.15 kW
- Total recorded battery capacity: 2,333 kWh
- Total recorded inverter capacity: 1,844.40 kW

These figures are arithmetic totals across the supplied records.

## 4. Notable Observations

### 4.1 Solar PV Capacity

Kuraye has the highest recorded PV capacity at 86.85 kWp.

Wawan Rafi has the lowest recorded PV capacity at 20.25 kWp.

This indicates variation in the recorded solar PV system configurations.

### 4.2 Battery Storage Capacity

The largest recorded battery capacity is 248 kWh.

This value occurs in both Kuraye and Bamuyayi.

The smallest recorded battery capacity is 60 kWh at Wawan Rafi.

### 4.3 Inverter Capacity

Kuraye has the highest recorded inverter capacity at 195.65 kW.

Wawan Rafi has the lowest recorded inverter capacity at 42.35 kW.

### 4.4 MPPT Capacity

The highest recorded MPPT capacity is 70.80 kW.

This value occurs in both Kuraye and Gadas.

### 4.5 Battery-to-PV Relationship

Across the 17 records, the battery-to-PV capacity ratios are relatively similar.

The calculated ratios range approximately from 2.82 to 2.96 kWh/kWp.

This suggests some consistency in the nominal battery storage capacity relative to PV capacity across these records.

However, this observation does not establish that the systems have identical operating requirements or performance.

## 5. Key Data Gaps

The initial dataset highlights several information gaps.

These include:

1. Unconfirmed measurement basis for average daily load.
2. Missing household population figures.
3. Missing geographical coordinates.
4. Missing grid electricity availability information.
5. Lack of actual PV generation records.
6. Lack of measured community electricity consumption.
7. Lack of battery operational and performance information.

These gaps limit the extent to which the adequacy of existing energy systems can be evaluated.

## 6. Preliminary Research Implications

The findings demonstrate the usefulness of organizing community renewable energy system information into a structured database.

The next stage should focus on obtaining additional information about actual energy consumption, grid availability, household numbers and operational system performance.

This will allow future analysis to move beyond system design capacity comparisons towards more meaningful assessments of community energy needs.

## 7. Conclusion

The initial dataset provides a foundation for further research into community renewable energy infrastructure along the Kaduna–Abuja corridor.

Although the available information is limited, it provides an opportunity to develop a structured approach to energy data collection, descriptive analysis and infrastructure research.

Future project updates will incorporate additional data, improved validation and more detailed analytical methods.
